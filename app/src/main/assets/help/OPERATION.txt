Background operation

The ongoing status notification reports whether the notification listener is connected, whether the grace timer is active, and whether Wi-Fi and Shizuku are ready. These are readiness indicators; a running service does not confirm that Meet exposed or accepted a decline action.

Android and device battery controls can stop or delay background work. A foreground service improves visibility but cannot guarantee uninterrupted protection.

The 24-hour grace timer

If active Wi-Fi or running, authorized Shizuku becomes unavailable, a saved 24-hour deadline starts. Restore both before the deadline to reset the timer.

When the deadline expires, protection turns off. Reconnect Wi-Fi, start and authorize Shizuku, then tap Enable protection. Reconnecting after expiry does not automatically enable protection.

The app and status notification display the countdown. Android may delay alarms, so QuietGate also checks expiry before call actions and when the app resumes.

Shizuku is a readiness condition. It does not supply a Meet call-control API or make rejection stronger. On non-rooted devices, Shizuku usually needs to be started again after a reboot.

The protection grace timer and Google contact snapshot freshness are separate 24-hour limits.
