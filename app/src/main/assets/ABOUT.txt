Purpose
Google saved contacts (0.9-alpha): optional per-account authorization and manual refresh of saved emails/numbers directly from Google. INTERNET and Google Play services Auth 22.0.0 are new. No Other contacts, Gmail, directory or contact names are requested. No QuietGate backend or caller-identifier upload for matching. Local AES-GCM encrypted, Keystore-protected keyed fingerprints allow matches for 24 hours; refresh pauses matching until successful. Account labels/status/cache are in app-private no-backup storage, with no tokens written to app files. Disconnect deletes a local cache and requests Google revocation; delete-all erases local account data and keys, with remaining Google grants managed separately. Cache/account data are not added to call exports. Google Cloud Android OAuth registration and People API enablement remain required; no live Google connection is verified. Google client libraries have separate SDK terms; complete dependency stack is not entirely FOSS. Full third-party notices are available from the Google contacts screen. See the release setup guide for limits and verification.

Incident reports (0.8-alpha): open Rejected-call catalog > Create incident report (ZIP). Select attempts, add incident notes and optional files, review and choose a destination. Reports include caller identities, UTC timestamps, categories/notes, imported labels, original attachment bytes and SHA-256 checksums. Up to 10 attachments, 10 MiB each / 25 MiB total. No added Android permissions. Files are unencrypted; selected cloud providers may upload exports/download attachments. Temporary app-private ZIPs are deleted on normal success/failure; process interruption can leave cache files until Android or the user clears app cache. Exports and original files must be deleted separately. Hashes do not authenticate identities or claims, establish independent time or prove chain of custody. No caller location/IP is obtained. Export work is not guaranteed to survive process termination.

A contact-based helper for unwanted Google Meet calls. It checks an exposed caller email or phone number against Contacts on this phone. Unknown or missing identifiers trigger an attempt to invoke Meet's own Decline/Reject action. Quiet mode can dismiss related missed-call alerts. It is independent software, not affiliated with or endorsed by Google or Shizuku.

Limits
This is experimental software, supplied without warranty. It cannot prove a caller is legitimate or a scammer, reveal hidden account information, block a call before Meet posts a notification, guarantee that no ringing or popup occurs, or confirm that Meet ended a call. It is not a cellular-call blocker. It depends on Meet exposing a usable notification/action, and English Decline/Reject labels are supported. Save the actual calling email; a phone-only contact does not establish that email's identity. Phone matching currently requires matching digits after punctuation removal. Work profiles, manufacturer restrictions, force-stop, reboot and future Meet changes can affect operation. Do not rely on it as your only protection.

The 24-hour grace timer
When either Wi-Fi or running, authorized Shizuku becomes unavailable, a saved 24-hour deadline starts. Restoring BOTH before that deadline resets the timer. At expiry protection switches off. Reconnect Wi-Fi, start and authorize Shizuku, then manually enable protection again. The app and status notification show the countdown. Android may delay background alarms or render the notification timer differently; the deadline is also checked before call actions and on app resume. Shizuku is an authorization/lease condition, not a Meet call-control API.

Why access is requested
Contacts: look up exposed caller emails/numbers locally. No contacts are edited.
Notification Access: Android grants broad notification-listener access; QuietGate's processing filters for Meet only. This access supplies call information and action buttons and allows related alert dismissal. Review this powerful access before enabling it.
Notifications: display the ongoing status/countdown notice.
Foreground service (special use): keep a visible, user-enabled status service. This does not guarantee survival.
Network state: determine whether Wi-Fi is the active connection. No IP address, SSID or hardware address is collected.
Boot completed: recheck the saved lease and request status restoration where Android permits it.
Shizuku authorization: check whether its service is running and authorizes QuietGate. QuietGate does not use it to query private account data or control other apps through privileged APIs.

Local data and deletion
INTERNET supports optional Google authorization and manual refresh. No telemetry, analytics, ads, silent account enumeration, device-ID collection, microphone, recording, location, call-log or phone-state access is requested by QuietGate. Caller matching stays local. Local decision history, grace/settings and bounded rejection fingerprints are separate from the connected-account cache. Clear local history & fingerprints removes call history/fingerprints, not connected-account data; disconnect/delete controls remove that cache. Uninstall removes app-private data but may leave Google-side grants until revoked. Google services necessarily process authorization/refresh requests under their own terms. Device contacts, Google accounts and Meet's history are not deleted by QuietGate. No private app data or signing keys are included in source distribution.

Free and open source
QuietGate source code and original assets are MIT licensed. Shizuku libraries are MIT; AndroidX and other open-source components have separate notices. Google Play services Auth and transitive Google libraries use the Android SDK License, with third-party notices available from the Google contacts screen. The complete dependency/runtime stack is not entirely FOSS. No F-Droid, FSF, Google Play or independent security certification is claimed. Google Meet, Android, Google services and Shizuku are external runtime services/components; build tools have their own terms.

Rejected-call catalog (0.6-alpha): stores Meet-exposed caller identities, attempt timestamps, and decline sent/failed status locally. These identities are not authenticated; action success is unconfirmed. Up to 500 entries from 90 days are retained, pruned on catalog access or write. No past identities can be reconstructed. JSON export uses the Android document picker and may contain personal data. Catalog Clear and Clear local history remove the app copy, not exports. No added permissions or automatic uploads. Google Other contacts may need saving as regular device contacts.

Catalog import/export (0.7-alpha): open Rejected-call catalog to import/export JSON, filter categories, and tap records to edit categories and notes. Labels are user assessments and do not change protection. Imports merge with existing records; duplicates keep existing edits and imported claims are labeled. Accepts versioned QuietGate JSON and legacy array exports up to 2 MB / 5,000 input records. Invalid, expired and future records are skipped. Local storage retains 500 newest entries within 90 days; export for longer archiving. Exports contain all retained categories, identities and notes. Choosing a cloud document provider can upload through that provider; choose local storage for local-only archives. No automatic uploads or new permissions.
